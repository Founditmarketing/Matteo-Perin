# Casa (/furniture) Design Review

*Four-specialist panel, 2026-07-01. 33 findings; the 12 high-impact ones below were each re-verified against the live page (0 refuted).*

## Confirmed high-impact findings

### 1. The Full Collection grid is the exact 'centered hero + card grid' template composition the house prohibits, and it spends a third of the page saying it

**Effort: large**

**Evidence:** src/components/FurnitureCollection.tsx:733-796 — centered heading 'Every Piece, a Statement' (line 746, text-center wrapper line 740), 6 filter pills (lines 26-33, rendered 757-771), and 17 identical aspect-[3/4] cards (line 659) in a uniform lg:grid-cols-3 grid (line 782). Measured live: the #collection section is 3,807px of an 11,403px desktop scroll at 1280x800 (33.4% of the page); at 375x812 it renders 17 single-column cards (first card 507px, average 493px) inside a 16,833px page (20.7 viewports tall), with the 6 filter pills wrapping into a 4-row wall of bordered chips. Each card carries a glassmorphic category chip (line 700, bg-white/10 backdrop-blur-sm; live computed backdrop-filter blur(4px)) and static faux carousel dots inside the image (lines 685-696) whose first dot is hardcoded active. DESIGN.md:169 prohibits 'centered hero + 3-col card grid + testimonial strip' as generic Shopify-luxe composition; DESIGN.md:174 verbatim bans glassmorphism; PRODUCT.md:28 says compose 'never template grids'. The recommendation's reuse targets are real: the contact-sheet band with caption 'Twelve views of one commission — select a frame to study it above.' is at CrocJacketLanding.tsx:481 (section 470-484), with the ledger plate device at 456-464.

**Recommendation:** Recompose the collection as editorial spreads in the site's own language: anchor pieces (Orfeo, Panorama, Sala Grande) get full-bleed or 2/3-width asymmetric moments with a ledger plate beside them; supporting pieces gather into the contact-sheet band pattern already built for CrocJacketLanding.tsx:470-482 ('Twelve views of one commission — select a frame to study it above'), one band per room category, replacing the filter pills. Kill the glass category chips and in-image dots; pace the 17 pieces like a World of Interiors portfolio — 3 acts with interstitials — instead of one undifferentiated wall.

### 2. A collector cannot meet any piece: no dimensions, no provenance, no edition, no commission terms anywhere on the page

**Effort: medium**

**Evidence:** src/components/FurnitureCollection.tsx:23 — `dimensions?: string` is declared but all 17 pieces (lines 35–216; 17 cards render live) omit it. Persistent card meta is name + 2 material chips (materials.slice(0,2), lines 711–717) plus a category tag on the image (699–703); a hover overlay (669–682) holds the tagline and a 2-line-clamped description, but it measures opacity:0 at 375×812 — on touch devices the description is unreachable entirely. Lightbox measured live at 1280×800: hasDimensions=false, hasDescription=false (piece.description is never rendered in the lightbox despite existing in data), hasPrice=false — full info text is 'Orfeo | HERITAGE LEATHER & CORDUROY | FULL-GRAIN COGNAC LEATHER | RIBBED CORDUROY | BRUSHED STEEL LEGS | INQUIRE ABOUT THIS PIECE'. Full-page keyword scan: price=false, edition=false, leadTime=false, №-numbering=false; the page's only commission language is generic CTAs ('Commission a Custom Piece' 916, 'Book a Consultation' 940) and one line saying pieces 'can be customized' (938) — no terms, lead times, or per-piece specifics. Meanwhile the overhauled Collection.tsx:313–318 gives every garment a ledger plate (№ 01 numbering in matteo-orange-ink) and CrocJacketLanding.tsx:428–429 sets real terms in the Italic Aside register ('$185,000 full commission... One of three commissions accepted each year'). Copy register drift confirmed: 'boutique hotel lobby' (115), 'Quilted Performance Fabric' (66), 'High-Resilience Core' (76) — against the provenance language already present in materialsData ('historic Biella mills', 805). One clarification for implementers: the Collection.tsx ledger plate carries a code comment that figures are deliberately withheld pending catalog re-verification (313–314), so the Casa ledger plates would extend, not copy, that device.

**Recommendation:** Give each piece the house's ledger plate: № number, name, dimensions (W×D×H), materials as a dossier list, and a lead-time / 'made to commission in Verona' line — on the card meta and expanded in the lightbox. Set the scarcity line in the Italic Aside register used on CrocJacketLanding.tsx:428 ('One of three commissions accepted each year'). Rewrite performance-fabric taglines into provenance language (mill names like the Biella reference already in materialsData:805). This is where the $185k-fluent buyer decides the furniture is the same hands as the jackets.

### 5. Per-piece inquiry path is invisible on touch: hover-only card overlay hides the gallery/inquiry affordance, and asking about a piece requires leaving the page

**Effort: medium**

**Evidence:** src/components/FurnitureCollection.tsx:669 gates the card's tagline, description, and 'View Full Gallery' cue behind isHovered; measured on the rendered page at 375x812 the overlay computes opacity: 0 (desktop hover confirms it only appears on mouseover, opacity 0 → 1), so a phone buyer sees a photo, a name, a small category tag, two 10px material labels, and ~6px indicator dots (w-1.5 h-1.5, line 690; rendered 6.6px on the scale-110 active dot) — none of which signals that tapping opens anything, and three pieces (Solenne, Cerchio, Accento, lines 181/191/201: empty galleryImages) show no dots at all. The only per-piece inquiry control is 'Inquire About This Piece' inside the lightbox (lines 1057-1064), which navigates to /bespoke and closes the lightbox (onClose, line 1061) — total path measured live: exactly 4,013px of scroll to reach #collection at 375x812 (page is 16,878px tall), tap card, tap inquire, then a client-side route change off /furniture that discards the gallery and scroll position. The recommendation's plumbing already exists: Collection.tsx:313-328 is the ledger-plate pattern (terracotta '№ 01' label, serif name, 44px 'Request this look' button calling openInquiry), Collection.tsx:67 uses useInquiry(), and InquiryProvider/InquiryModal are mounted globally in App.tsx:344/359, so an in-place, piece-pre-filled inquiry on /furniture is a straight reuse.

**Recommendation:** Reuse the house's ledger-plate pattern from Collection.tsx:313-328 — under each card, a terracotta '№ 01'-style plate with the piece name and a 44px 'Request this piece' text link that calls the existing useInquiry() context (Collection.tsx:67) to open InquiryModal in place, pre-filled with the piece. This makes tap-to-ask a one-tap act, keeps the buyer on the page, and finally gives Casa the numbered-ledger scarcity register the rest of the overhaul speaks.

### 6. Lightbox ignores swipe on mobile — the universal gallery gesture does nothing; navigation depends on arrow chips sitting on top of the photograph

**Effort: small**

LightboxModal (FurnitureCollection.tsx:954-1069) contains no touch/drag/pan handlers — a grep for onTouch|drag|swipe|PanInfo|onPan across the whole 1,146-line file matches only draggable={false} at line 882 (materials band). The only in-viewport navigation is two 48x48px arrow buttons measured at x=8 and x=319 (w-12 h-12, left-2/right-2 at lines 1004-1021), both overlapping the 343px-wide image (image measured x=16–359, arrows span x=8–56 and x=319–367, vertically centered on the photo at y=223–271), plus 44px dot targets below (lines 1038-1055). Recommendation stands as written — framer-motion is already imported at line 2 and goNext/goPrev exist at lines 958-959; a drag="x" wrapper with an onDragEnd threshold and a 700ms ease-out-expo settle matches DESIGN.md's motion register (line 165) — with one citation correction: receding the arrow chips off the photograph is justified by PRODUCT.md design principle 2 ("The garment is the hero — chrome recedes"), not The Lifted Print Rule, which is DESIGN.md's shadow rule (line 128).

**Recommendation:** Wrap the lightbox image in a framer-motion drag="x" container (framer is already the page's motion library, line 2) with an onDragEnd threshold calling the existing goNext/goPrev (lines 958-959), with a subtle slide-settle in the house's 700ms ease-out-expo register. On mobile, the arrow chips can then recede (or move off the photograph) per The Lifted Print Rule — chrome should not sit on the print.

### 11. All 17 collection-grid images load eagerly from 4,500px below the fold — 1.72MB fetched on arrival, competing with the hero video

**Effort: small**

**Evidence:** FurnitureCollection.tsx:665 sets loading="eager" on the raw <img> (lines 661-666) inside every CollectionCard; the COLLECTION array (lines 35-216) contains 17 pieces. Measured on the live page (puppeteer, cache disabled, no scrolling): 17 of 17 card images sit below the fold at load — the first grid row is at y=4,592px on a 1280x800 viewport (5.7 viewports down) and y=4,451px on 375x812 — yet all 17 -md.webp files download immediately at both sizes, totaling 1,727,982 bytes. Total cold-load transfer with zero scrolling measured 9,810,478 bytes at 1280x800 and 9,243,597 bytes at 375x812 (the finding's 10.09MB/9.67MB figures are within 3-5%, run-to-run video range-request variance); the eager cards are ~17.6% of the desktop total. The recommendation is sound: ResponsiveImage.tsx:26 defaults loading to "lazy", so EditorialSplit (FurnitureCollection.tsx:499), ParallaxImage/CinematicGallery (line 564, explicit lazy), MaterialsShowcase (line 881, explicit lazy), and CinematicInterstitial (line 908, default lazy) are all lazy already — the collection grid is the only eager loader on the page, and its whileInView reveal (lines 653-655) has identical initial/visible states, so lazy-loading changes nothing visually.

**Recommendation:** Change loading="eager" to "lazy" (or simply drop the attribute — lazy is what the rest of the page uses). The page's own EditorialSplit, CinematicGallery, and MaterialsShowcase images are already lazy via ResponsiveImage and the RevealSection/whileInView cadence hides any late arrival behind the existing slow ease-out reveal, so nothing visually changes — the browser's native threshold will have each row decoded well before it enters the buyer's viewport.
